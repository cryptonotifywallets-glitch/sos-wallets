📧 Email Delivery Setup

Want recipients to actually receive email notifications? SOS WALLETS is a client-side app, so it needs a free EmailJS account to send real emails. Set it up once and every notification is delivered automatically — no mailto clicks needed.

📖 Step-by-step EmailJS setup guide (takes ~5 minutes, free)

🔒 First — keep your personal email private

The address that appears as the "From" sender in recipients' inboxes is whatever email account you connect to EmailJS as a Service. If you connect your personal Gmail, recipients will see your personal Gmail address.

To keep your personal email private, create a dedicated/branded sender email and connect THAT to EmailJS instead. For example:

  • Create a new free Gmail account like soswallets.notify@gmail.com or yourbrand.alerts@gmail.com and connect that one.
  • Or — best for branding — use a custom domain (e.g. notify@yourbrand.com) connected via Gmail (Settings → Accounts → "Send mail as") or via EmailJS's custom SMTP option.

Then set a Sender Display Name below (e.g. "SOS WALLETS" or "GlobalPay Notifications") so recipients see a clean branded name in their inbox, not a random address.

🌐 Best option — use your own custom domain (e.g. notify@yourbrand.com)

If you buy your own domain (~$9–11/yr for a .com at Porkbun, Namecheap, or Cloudflare), you can send from a fully branded address like notify@yourbrand.com. Recipients see your brand, never a Gmail address, and never your personal email. Two ways to do it:

  • Free route (recommended): Set up free email forwarding with ImprovMX (free tier) — it forwards notify@yourbrand.com to a Gmail account you control. Then in Gmail, enable "Send mail as" notify@yourbrand.com (Settings → Accounts → "Add another email address", using ImprovMX's SMTP). Finally connect THAT Gmail to EmailJS. Recipients see notify@yourbrand.com.
  • Direct SMTP route: In EmailJS, add a service and choose Custom SMTP instead of Gmail. Enter your domain's SMTP server + port + username + password (e.g. from ImprovMX SMTP, or any paid mail host like Zoho/Google Workspace). EmailJS sends directly through your domain.

Either way, you still use the same Service ID / Template ID / Public Key / Sender Display Name fields below — nothing in this app changes. A custom domain just makes the "From" address look fully professional and 100% hides your personal email.

📋 Full setup walkthrough

  1. Create your dedicated sender email first (see above) — e.g. sign up for a fresh Gmail at mail.google.com and name it something like yourbrand.notify. This is the address recipients will see. Don't use your personal email.
  2. Go to dashboard.emailjs.com/sign-up and create a free account (200 emails/month free, no card needed).
  3. In the EmailJS dashboard, click Email Services → Add New Service. Choose Gmail (or Outlook/Yahoo/Custom SMTP). When prompted, sign in with your dedicated sender email (the one from step 1, not your personal email). Click "Create Service" and copy the Service ID shown (looks like service_xxxxxxx).
  4. Click Email Templates → Create New Template. Set these fields exactly:
    • To Email: {to_email}
    • From Name: {from_name} (so the display name you set below shows up, instead of a raw email address)
    • Subject: {subject}
    • Content: Click the "HTML" toggle at the top of the content editor, then paste {html} into the body. (If you prefer plain text, use {message} instead.)
    Scroll down and click Save. Copy the Template ID (looks like template_xxxxxxx).
  5. On the left sidebar click Account, then scroll to API Keys. Copy your Public Key (looks like user_xxxxxxxxxxxxx or a long alphanumeric string).
  6. Back here in SOS WALLETS: paste all three IDs below, type a Sender Display Name (e.g. SOS WALLETS), and click Save.
  7. Enter your own email in the "Test email recipient" field and click Send Test Email. Check your inbox (and spam folder). You should see it arrive from your dedicated sender email with the display name you chose.

🔒 Your keys + display name are stored only in your browser (localStorage). EmailJS public keys are safe to use client-side — that's how they're designed to work. Never paste your private/secret key here — EmailJS only needs the public one.
